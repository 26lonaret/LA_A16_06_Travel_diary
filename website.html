A travel note from Nepal

Kathmandu first.
The mountains when
you're ready.

Let the first day stay loose. Wander the old squares, stop for tea when you need a break, and look up: the carved windows are easy to miss if you hurry.

Start exploring ↓

Three places to begin

Don't try to see it all in a day.

01 / WALK

Kathmandu Valley

Choose one or two old squares, then follow whichever lane catches your eye. There are plenty of carved windows to miss if you rush.

02 / PAUSE

Boudhanath

Walk a slow loop, then find a rooftop tea stall and watch the light change. Mornings tend to feel quieter.

03 / WANDER

Everest region

The views are huge, but getting there takes time. Build in days to travel and adjust to the altitude instead of squeezing it into a tight schedule.

A route with breathing room

Leave space between stops.

First days

Stay in the Kathmandu Valley long enough to get your bearings. Pick a neighborhood each day rather than racing between sights.

One morning

Go to Boudhanath early, take a loop around the stupa, and give yourself time to sit down afterward.

After that

If you're heading toward Everest, allow real time for the journey, weather, and acclimatization. It's a trip of its own.

A few small notes

A few things make the trip easier.

At temples

Dress respectfully and check local customs. Ask before taking someone's photograph.

On the trail

Choose a route that fits your experience, and follow current local advice about weather and altitude.

Leave a buffer

Travel days can take longer than expected. A spare afternoon is often better than a packed schedule.
